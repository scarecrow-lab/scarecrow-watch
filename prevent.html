Seven signs that a country is moving toward mass atrocities

PREVENT is a risk assessment framework from the Auschwitz Institute for the Prevention of Genocide and Mass Atrocities (AIPG). It groups the warning signs into seven categories, one per letter, and leans on indicators that can be measured, so anyone can apply it to any country.

Framework by AIPG. This page is Scarecrow's plain-language summary; it is not an official AIPG publication.

The big idea

Dry field, then a spark

Atrocities rarely come from nowhere. Six categories describe slow-moving conditions that build up over years, like a field drying out. The seventh describes sudden events that can set it alight. What decides the outcome is how those in power respond.

The seven categories

What each letter looks at

Chips with a dashed border are things a country cannot change, such as its history or geography. They still raise risk, but prevention concentrates on the rest.

P

Prior atrocities & human rights abuses

Has this happened here before? Past mass violence, past armed conflict and past widespread abuse.

Past atrocitiesPast armed conflictPast widespread abuses
The strongest single predictor. History can't be undone, but truth-telling, justice and redress can weaken its pull.
R

Regime upheaval

Is power changing hands by force or outside the rules?

Armed conflictRevolutionSuccessful coupAssassination of a leaderOther unlawful takeovers
The next biggest predictor after history: new rulers who took power by force often use force to keep it.
E

Executive overreach & non-democratic rule

Can anyone stop the government from doing what it wants?

Weak state (infant mortality)Electoral democracyUnlimited executive powerCourts under pressureMedia censorshipFactionalismRegime durabilityWomen in parliament
Mixed regimes, neither full democracies nor full autocracies, tend to carry the highest risk.
V

Volatility in the economy & environment

Is daily life getting harder, and is the hardship shared unfairly?

Low developmentEconomic downturnsEconomic discrimination between groupsClimate stress & displacement
The type of economy matters too: tourism-based economies tend to lower risk, while oil and mining economies do not.
E

Exclusion & social fragmentation

Are some groups treated as outsiders by law, by ideology or by those in power?

Exclusionary ideologyState-led discriminationElite drawn from one ethnic groupLimits on women in politicsLimits on religious freedomPopulation size, density & growth
Exclusion is how a group gets marked as a target long before violence starts.
N

Neighbouring instability & international factors

Is violence spilling over the border, and how connected is the country to the world?

Violence in neighbouring countriesWithin ~200 km of the borderTrade opennessGeography & history
Countries that trade more with the world tend to face lower risk; conflict close to the border raises it.
T

Threats to those in power & triggers

What could set things off in the coming months? These are events, not conditions, so they are watched separately.

Anti-government protests & actionAttempted coups (especially in the last 5 years)Attempted revolutionsUpcoming or disputed electionsPeace talksInternational pressure
The event itself is not the danger. The danger is a regime that answers it by turning on a group of people. International pressure can push either way.
Where prevention works

What can change, and what can't

PREVENT separates indicators that policy can move from those it can't. Fixed factors explain why a country starts out at higher risk; changeable ones are where governments, civil society and partners can actually lower it.

Can change

  • Checks on executive power, independent courts, a free press
  • Discrimination in law and in the economy
  • Women's political participation and religious freedom
  • How the state responds to protests, elections and coups
  • Justice and redress for past abuses

Fixed or very slow

  • A history of atrocities or war
  • Geography and neighbours
  • Population size, density and growth

How we turn PREVENT into a score

AIPG's framework names the categories and indicators but does not set scales, weights or cut-offs. The scoring below is Scarecrow's own adaptation, published so anyone can check it.

Every indicator is rated 1 to 5
1Low
2Some
3Moderate
4High
5Severe

Example of an automated cut-off: infant mortality, a common measure of state weakness, scores 1 below 10 deaths per 1,000 births and 5 at 55 or more. All cut-offs live in the open data file data/prevent.json on GitHub.
