Remembrance

Documented mass-atrocity events against the Rohingya, drawn from public investigations by the UN and human-rights organizations. We record places, dates and verified findings — to resist erasure — not the personal data of individuals, and we show no graphic images.

The UN Fact-Finding Mission gave a conservative estimate that at least 10,000 Rohingya were killed in the two months after August 2017. Each event below is one documented part of that whole. UN Fact-Finding Mission ↗
Min Gyi (Tula Toli), Maungdaw 30 Aug 2017
UN investigators estimate around 750 Rohingya were killed, and the village was burned to the ground. The Fact-Finding Mission described it as a well-planned, deliberate attack on a specific civilian population.
Source: Human Rights Watch ↗
Chut Pyin, Rathedaung 27 Aug 2017
Physicians for Human Rights documented an estimated 400 villagers killed or missing, including 99 children, and a group of 50 men who were taken away and never seen again.
Source: Physicians for Human Rights ↗
Gu Dar Pyin, Buthidaung 27 Aug 2017
After soldiers overran the village, survivors described several mass graves. Village elders recorded 75 names; local estimates ran as high as 400 killed. First documented by the Associated Press in 2018.
Source: Anadolu Agency / HRW ↗
Inn Din, Maungdaw Sep 2017
An initial survey counted 147 Inn Din villagers killed; Reuters documented the execution of ten detained men. Two Reuters journalists were jailed for reporting the killings.
Source: Fortify Rights (ReliefWeb) ↗
About this record. Estimates differ between investigations; each event links to its source. This records events and communities, not a registry of individuals. Records are kept in English, linked to the original investigations, for accuracy.

Part of Scarecrow Watch — an independent, non-profit, open-source early-warning watch for Southeast Asia. This page documents public findings and holds no personal data.
